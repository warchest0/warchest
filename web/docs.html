01 / How the treasury builds

Warchest is a fixed-supply ERC20. Its token contract has no transfer-tax logic. A custom Uniswap v4 hook collects a 10% fee on buys and sells through the configured pool, always in ETH. Ordinary token transfers and liquidity operations are not taxed.

Fees accumulate as ERC-6909 claims in the hook and are flushed to the vault. The vault converts ETH to USDG through a bounded swap, then can bridge approved capital to HyperEVM via Across. The multisig completes funding of the Hyperliquid trading account.

A continuous cycle

  1. Swaps generate fees for the treasury.
  2. Daily snapshots compute the community’s voting weight.
  3. A valid vote selects an eligible asset and direction.
  4. The vault authorizes a bounded allocation; the keeper executes the position.
  5. Positions close under their protection rules or a valid close request. Return of funds requires the multisig.

Profit distribution versus buyback and burn remains an open decision (D7). The distribution module is disabled by default. The site makes no promise of yield or automatic payouts.

02 / Influence grows with time

Each received token lot begins at level 0 on its acquisition day. Its level rises by one per full UTC day, capped at 10. The off-chain indexer computes this from the token’s transfer history.

Voting weight = Σ (lot amount × min(10, whole days held))

The homepage simulator shows one lot of 1,000 tokens. For example, 7 full days produces a weight of 7,000; 10 or more days produces 10,000. This multiplier is voting influence, not an investment return.

Seniority follows the lots

Selling or transferring tokens consumes the newest lots first (LIFO). Older unsold lots keep their seniority. A receiving wallet gets a new level-0 lot; a self-transfer does not reset seniority.

Snapshots, votes and fallback

The daily weight is committed through a Merkle root with a challenge window. Each holder votes once per round using their snapshot proof. Votes choose an asset and long/short direction from the configured eligible list. A close vote is available once its conditions are met.

If quorum fails or the outcome ties, the previous decision remains. A position stopped out is never automatically reopened: a new valid decision is required.

03 / Rules and their limits

  • Allocation cap: one position can use at most 20% of the vault’s liquid treasury value. The deployment can choose a lower cap.
  • Separate responsibilities: governance holds no funds. The vault holds capital and executes only validated decisions.
  • Bounded conversion: ETH-to-USDG swaps use on-chain time-weighted price safeguards and slippage bounds.
  • Trading-only keeper: the agent signs allowed trading actions. Withdrawals and return movements require the multisig. Funded testnet evidence for remaining permission edge cases is still pending.
  • Position protection: the keeper places and reads back stop orders on Hyperliquid. The vault itself cannot enforce a stop on another chain.
  • Guardian: can pause and challenge, but cannot choose a winning vote or redirect the vault’s bridge recipient.

What remains exposed

Market losses, execution risk, bridge risk and operational trust remain. A compromised trading agent can lose funds through trading even without withdrawal rights. The vault currently has no general exit or migration path. These limits are documented in the repository and need to be assessed before deployment.
